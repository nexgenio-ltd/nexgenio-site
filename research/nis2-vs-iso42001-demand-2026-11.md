## PART 2 — WHAT THE MARKET BUYS INSTEAD (the implementation credential)

**There is no EU-wide or national NIS2 certification scheme for organisations.** Every competent-authority source found says this explicitly:

- **Germany (BSI)**: ISO/IEC 27001 certification is explicitly **not** treated as proof or precondition of NIS2/BSIG compliance. BSI prescribes no specific standard; ISO 27001 "can be considered" but must be individually evaluated within risk management. Source: https://www.bsi.bund.de/DE/Themen/Regulierte-Wirtschaft/NIS-2-regulierte-Unternehmen/NIS-2-Infopakete/NIS-2-ISO27001/NIS-2-ISO-27001.html and FAQ: https://www.bsi.bund.de/DE/Themen/Regulierte-Wirtschaft/NIS-2-regulierte-Unternehmen/NIS-2-FAQ/NIS-2-FAQ-allgemein/FAQ-zu-NIS-2.html
- **Netherlands (NCSC)**: No official Dutch certification pathway for Cyberbeveiligingswet/NIS2. Neither NIS2 nor the Cbw requires ISO 27001 or NEN 7510 certification; those are reference frameworks only. Sources: https://www.ncsc.nl/cyberbeveiligingswet-nis2 ; https://www.pentrox.nl/en/nis2-pentest/ (secondary, states the "no certificate required" point explicitly).
- **Belgium (CCB)** is the one country with an **official, named conformity-assessment pathway**: CyberFundamentals (**CyFun®**) at Basic/Important (self-verification) or Essential (certification by an accredited CAB), **or** ISO/IEC 27001 certification with CCB-acceptable scope and Statement of Applicability. This is the closest thing in the eight markets to an "implementation credential" — but it certifies the **organisation's ISMS**, not an individual's professional credential. Sources: https://cyfun.eu/en/cabs/cabs-belgium ; CCB FAQ: https://ccb.belgium.be/sites/default/files/2025-02/NIS2%20FAQ%20Website%20v2.0.1%20EN.pdf
- **ENISA**: Technical Implementation Guidance maps NIS2 risk-management requirements to ISO/IEC 27001 and other standards as an aid, explicitly stating the guidance is **not legally binding** and does not replace national frameworks. ISO 27001 helps as an evidence base; it is not a stated substitute for compliance. Source: https://www.enisa.europa.eu/publications/nis2-technical-implementation-guidance and https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025-06/ENISA_Technical_implementation_guidance_on_cybersecurity_risk_management_measures_version_1.0.pdf
- **Austria** is the one market with a **named personal certification scheme explicitly branded "NIS2"**: Austrian Standards' "Zertifizierter NIS2-Officer" (ISO/IEC 17024 person certification, 36-question/45-minute exam, 3-year validity). It is **voluntary**, not a statutory requirement, and is a national/Austrian Standards product, not an EU-recognised credential. Source: https://www.austrian-standards.at/de/produkte-loesungen/standards-anwenden/zertifizierung/personenzertifizierungen/nis2-officer
- **PECB's "NIS 2 Directive Lead Implementer"** is a real, marketed credential (ISO/IEC 17024-based, 5 years' experience + 2 years cybersecurity management required, 3-year validity) but no evidence was found of it being named by employers or by any competent authority as a recognised implementation pathway. It is PECB's own positioning, not corroborated by an authority or employer demand signal. Source: https://pecb.com/en/education-and-certification-for-individuals/nis-2-directive/nis-2-directive-lead-implementer

**Analyst/skills-gap commentary** (ISACA, ISC2, ENISA NIS Investments report, SANS 2026 workforce survey) consistently frames the gap as a **skills/training gap**, not a missing-certificate gap: ENISA-linked coverage states 76% of staff in NIS2-scope organisations lack certified training *relevant to compliance*; SANS 2026 survey says regulation-driven hiring impact jumped from 40% (2025) to 95% (2026) with NIS2 the single largest driver (30%). This supports training demand broadly, but does not specify which certificate (if any) buyers are converging on. Sources: https://www.isc2.org/Insights/2024/11/How-NIS2-Will-Impact-You-and-Your-Organization ; https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-02/NIS%20Investments%202025%20-%20Main%20report.pdf ; https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/07/22/cybersecurity-workforce-trends-report/

**Verdict for Part 2**: The market's de facto "implementation credential" — where one exists at all — is **ISO 27001 (organisational certification)**, explicitly recognised in Belgium's CCB framework and referenced (but not mandated) by ENISA, BSI and NCSC. No market in the eight has converged on a NIS2-specific *personal* credential as a recognised hiring or compliance signal, except Austria's own voluntary scheme, which has no demonstrated employer pull in job ads.

## PART 5 — THE COMPETING SUBJECT: ISO 42001 / AI GOVERNANCE

- **Job-ad demand**: Real but described as "niche" — concentrated in consultant/practitioner/auditor listings (ComplyIndex directories showing dozens of named practitioners in Austria, Netherlands, Germany) rather than high-volume corporate job postings. Indeed shows on the order of tens of open "ISO 42001" roles in Germany (~50 per one job-board snapshot). Source: https://complyindex.com/iso-42001/austria/ ; https://complyindex.com/iso-42001/netherlands/ ; https://de.indeed.com/q-iso-42001-jobs.html
- **What's driving it**: Listings consistently pair ISO 42001 with **EU AI Act readiness**, not with customer-contract requirements as the stated driver in the sources found. Source: https://complyindex.com/iso-42001/
- **EU AI Act timeline — important correction to the "August 2026" assumption**: The AI Act's high-risk obligations have been **delayed by the Digital Omnibus amendments**. GPAI obligations began August 2025 (already past). High-risk obligations under Article 6(2)/Annex III (the bulk of standalone high-risk use cases — hiring, education, credit scoring, etc.) now apply from **2 December 2027**, not August 2026. High-risk systems embedded in regulated products (Article 6(1)/Annex I) move to **2 August 2028**. This means the regulatory clock pushing ISO 42001 demand is **further out than NIS2's**, not closer. Source: https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/EU-AI-act-amendments-defer-and-clarify-obligations ; https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai
- **Search interest**: No Google Trends data obtainable (same limitation as Part 3). Qualitative signal is growing adoption language (Google Cloud, Google Workspace, Gemini certified against ISO 42001) and reporting that certification is spreading into public sector and financial institutions — but this is adoption/PR commentary, not a verified search-volume trend. Source: https://cloud.google.com/security/compliance/iso-42001

**Verdict — NIS2 vs ISO 42001, which is stronger right now, which is rising faster**:
- **Stronger right now**: **NIS2.** It has active, dated legal obligations already biting in 6 of 8 markets (DE, AT, NL, BE, LU, LI), a measurable job-ad footprint using the term "NIS2" directly in titles and descriptions, and analyst-quantified training-demand growth (SANS: regulation-driven hiring impact 40%→95% 2025→2026, NIS2 the single largest driver at 30%).
- **Rising faster near-term**: Also **NIS2**, precisely because its compliance deadlines are live now (Belgium April 2026/2027, Austria ~Jan 2027) while the AI Act's comparable high-risk deadlines have just been pushed out to Dec 2027/Aug 2028. ISO 42001 demand is real and growing but is currently consultant/practitioner-led rather than broad corporate hiring, and its regulatory forcing function is now further away in time than NIS2's.
- **Caveat on the decision this feeds**: Neither finding addresses the colleague's core challenge — whether buyers want a **credential** (PECB NIS2 Lead Implementer specifically) versus whether they want **NIS2 competence/compliance help** generally. The evidence in Parts 1–3 supports the latter, not the former: no job ad found names a NIS2-specific personal certificate, and the dominant market pattern (ISO 27001/CISSP/CISM as "nice to have," CCB's CyFun-or-ISO27001 organisational pathway, BSI/NCSC explicitly declining to endorse any certificate) treats NIS2 as a compliance *subject* to be taught alongside general security credentials, not as a certifiable destination in its own right.
