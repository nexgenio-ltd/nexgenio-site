Your pre-DORA classification does not transfer

Five jurisdictions rated three different objects before DORA. Part of that work carries straight into the register of information. Most of it shows you where to look and decides nothing.

The target test

What the critical or important function test actually asks

Everything a supervisor now expects about criticality resolves to one definition, in Article 3(22) of DORA, and to a small number of register fields that force an answer in writing.

“Critical or important function” means a function, the disruption of which would materially impair the financial performance of a financial entity, or the soundness or continuity of its services and activities, or the discontinued, defective or failed performance of that function would materially impair the continuing compliance of a financial entity with the conditions and obligations of its authorisation, or with its other obligations under applicable financial services law.

Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, Article 3(22)

Three limbs, joined by or. Any one of them is enough. The object being rated is a function, not a system, not a contract and not a dataset. That single point is where most legacy work stops being reusable.

The register of information turns the definition into fields you have to populate, under Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2956.

FieldWhat it asks for
B_06.01.0060Criticality or importance assessment. A closed set of options, so there is no partial answer
B_06.01.0070Reasons for criticality or importance, in 300 characters
B_06.01.0080Date of the last assessment of criticality or importance, as an ISO 8601 date
B_06.01.0090Recovery time objective of the function, in hours
B_06.01.0100Recovery point objective of the function, in hours
B_02.02.0170Sensitiveness of the data stored by the ICT third-party service provider
B_02.02.0180Level of reliance on the ICT service supporting the critical or important function
B_07.01.0050Substitutability of the ICT third-party service provider
B_07.01.0100Impact of discontinuing the ICT services

Field B_06.01.0080 is the quiet one. It asks for a date, which means the assessment has to exist as a dated act rather than as a view held by the team. Most legacy classification work has no date attached to it.

The history

What each supervisor asked for before DORA

Nothing here was idle. Every jurisdiction had a classification duty, and several ran for the better part of a decade. The question is not whether firms classified things. It is what they classified.

2017
BaFin issues BAIT, Rundschreiben 10/2017. German institutions must establish the protection requirements of the components of their Informationsverbund.
2018
VAIT follows for insurers. The FMA circulates two draft IT-Sicherheit Leitfäden in Austria.
2019
The EBA publishes the revised outsourcing Guidelines in February, defining a critical or important function, and the ICT and security risk management Guidelines in November, requiring criticality classification of information assets.
2020
CSSF Circular 20/750 in Luxembourg. EIOPA issues its cloud outsourcing Guidelines for insurers.
2021
ZAIT for payment institutions. ESMA issues its cloud outsourcing Guidelines. The Central Bank of Ireland publishes cross-industry guidance on outsourcing and on operational resilience.
2022
CSSF Circular 22/806 replaces the Luxembourg outsourcing framework. DORA is adopted in December.
16 January 2025
VAIT, ZAIT and KAIT expire.
17 January 2025
DORA applies.
31 December 2026
BAIT expires in full, having run on in reduced form through the transition.

Why the German dates are not interchangeable

Three of the four German IT circulars expired at the end of 16 January 2025. BAIT did not. A recast BAIT with reduced scope entered force on 17 January 2025 and runs to the end of 2026. Anyone citing a single withdrawal date for all four is citing the wrong one for BAIT.

The core distinction

Three objects, and only one of them is a function

Pre-DORA schemes rated one of three different things. Keeping them apart is the whole exercise, because they are not degrees of the same judgement. They are answers to different questions.

Object one

ICT asset or information

Rated for protection need against confidentiality, integrity, availability and in the German and Austrian texts authenticity as well.

This is BAIT, VAIT, ZAIT and the EBA ICT Guidelines. A property of a thing you hold.

Object two

Contractual arrangement

Rated material or not material, which decided whether a heavier outsourcing regime applied to the contract.

This is MaRisk AT 9, the CSSF circulars, the CBI outsourcing guidance and the EBA outsourcing Guidelines. A property of a deal.

Object three

Business function or service

Rated critical or important by reference to the harm its disruption would do.

This is the object DORA rates. Only the European guidelines and the Luxembourg and Irish texts reached it directly.

The error this prevents

A high protection rating on an asset is not a critical function, and a low one does not clear it. An asset can carry the highest confidentiality rating a firm issues and support nothing critical. A critical function can run on assets with modest confidentiality needs. Protection need maps to the data sensitivity and recovery fields of the register. It never maps to B_06.01.0060.

The translation

How the legacy levels actually map

Four patterns cover every scheme on the grid. The register field is what makes each one concrete, so every row says what you may carry forward and what is still open.

1. A binary flag under the same test

EBA/GL/2019/02, CSSF 22/806, EIOPA Guideline 7, and the Austrian population working to the EBA text.

What you holdCarries toStill open
Function flagged critical or importantB_06.01.0060 as it stands, with B_06.01.0070 re-expressed against the three limbsConfirm the scope has not moved, and supply B_06.01.0080 where the legacy file carries no date
Function flagged not criticalNothingA full re-test. The legacy exercise looked at functions that were outsourced. DORA looks at all of them

That asymmetry is the expensive line on this page. The positives transfer. None of the negatives do.

2. A binary flag under a differently scoped test

SchemeRelationship to the DORA setWhat follows
ESMA cloud GuidelinesA subset. ESMA refers to the main services and activities. Article 3(22) refers to its services and activities, with no qualifierFunctions sitting behind secondary services are absent and have to be found
CBI outsourcing guidanceA superset. The methodology is the firm's own and board approved, and it weighs a financial stability factor that Article 3(22) does not apply to a single entityA function on the list only for stability reasons is not a DORA critical function. Prune rather than extend
CBI operational resilience guidanceA different object. A business service is external facing by definition. A function is internalThe service list is the top of the chain. The DORA functions are the internal links beneath it

3. A materiality flag on an arrangement

MaRisk AT 9, and the Austrian materiality test under section 25 of the Bankwesengesetz.

Legacy levelWhat it indicatesWhat closes the gap
MaterialThe function behind the arrangement is worth assessing. No limb is engaged, because none of these texts names limbsA fresh Article 3(22) assessment per function, documented against each of the three limbs
Not materialNothing either wayThe same assessment. MaRisk leaves the criteria to the institution, so two firms can classify one arrangement oppositely and both comply

Austria differs on evidence rather than on the test. Because section 25(5) of the Bankwesengesetz required prior notification of every material IT outsourcing to the FMA, an Austrian institution holds a dated filing that went to the regulator for each arrangement it called material. That is the closest thing to a legacy answer for B_06.01.0080 anywhere in this set.

4. A protection need on an asset

BAIT, VAIT, ZAIT and the EBA ICT and security risk Guidelines. This pattern maps sideways, never up.

Legacy ratingCarries toNever to
Protection need, confidentialityB_02.02.0170, the sensitiveness of the data being processedB_06.01.0060
Protection need, availabilityB_06.01.0090 and B_06.01.0100, the recovery time and recovery point objectivesB_06.01.0060
Protection class on an end-user applicationB_02.02.0170, and it is where undocumented dependencies usually sitB_06.01.0060

BAIT names four protection objectives and no levels at all. The categories every German institution uses are its own invention, which is why two firms in the same business can hold incomparable ratings and both satisfy BAIT. The register does not accept that, because B_06.01.0060 is a closed set of options.

By jurisdiction

Five starting positions

What each supervisor built, and what a firm in that jurisdiction is likely to be holding today.

JurisdictionWhere it startedCarry-over
European guidelines The EBA defined a critical or important function in February 2019 for outsourcing purposes, then addressed information asset classification separately nine months later. DORA kept the first object and left the levels of the second to the firm. Strong on functions, indicative on assets
Germany BaFin rated information rather than functions. Four protection objectives, no categories named in the circular, and a materiality test the institution wrote for itself. Three of the four circulars expired on 16 January 2025 and BAIT runs to the end of 2026. Weak. The object is wrong and the criteria were never prescribed
Austria Austria never finalised national IT guidance of its own, and what the FMA publishes for ICT risk and outsourcing is the EBA text in German translation. The Austrian contribution is statutory instead: a materiality flag in section 25 of the Bankwesengesetz and a prior notification to the FMA for every material IT outsourcing. Strong by adoption for EBA scope firms, and the best dated evidence in the set
Luxembourg The CSSF came closest. Circular 22/806 defines a critical or important function in terms DORA reuses almost unchanged, and the CSSF has since rescoped Circular 20/750 to the entities DORA does not reach. Strong. A 22/806 register maps straight across
Ireland The Central Bank rated the business service rather than the function, and asked who the external end user was. A different question, and a sharper one than any limb of Article 3(22). Partial on both counts, over-inclusive on outsourcing
Our assessment

Which test is the better instrument

Everything above this heading is quoted from the instruments themselves. What follows is our judgement, offered as such.

Verdict

DORA is the better supervisory instrument and the weaker internal risk tool.

DimensionBetterWhy
ComparabilityDORAOne definition across every jurisdiction. A group with entities in four of them previously ran four incompatible tests on three different objects.
FalsifiabilityDORAMaRisk and the Austrian materiality test both leave the criteria to the institution. A test whose criteria the tested party writes cannot be failed, so it could not be compared across a market.
Named criteriaDORAArticle 3(22) names three limbs. BAIT names a method and no categories.
GranularityLegacySeveral legacy schemes graded protection need across four levels. DORA is binary, and a binary flag across a register of several hundred rows tends to collapse toward one side.
Asset protection needLegacyBAIT, VAIT and ZAIT all rated information on confidentiality, integrity, availability and authenticity. Article 3(22) rates no assets at all. That work moved to Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/1774, so criticality and protection need now sit in separate instruments with nothing joining them.
Timeliness of evidenceLegacy, AustriaSection 25(5) of the Bankwesengesetz put the assessment in front of the FMA before signature. The register of information is annual and looks backwards.
Customer harmLegacy, IrelandThe Central Bank asked who the external end user was. No limb of Article 3(22) asks that, and it is the sharpest question in the whole pre-DORA set.

The honest version

Four to three is a split, not a win, which is why the verdict is phrased the way it is. DORA tells a supervisor which arrangements matter across a market. It does not tell a firm which of its own systems to protect first, and three of the schemes it replaced did exactly that.

Questions

Frequently asked

Can we reuse our BAIT protection need ratings for the register of information?
For some fields, yes. The confidentiality rating supports the data sensitiveness field, and the availability rating supports the recovery time and recovery point objectives. It cannot support the criticality assessment field, because protection need is a property of an asset and criticality is a property of a function. The two were always different objects, and BAIT never claimed otherwise.
Our outsourcing register already flags material arrangements. Is that our critical function list?
No, for two reasons. A material arrangement is a contract and a critical function is a function, so the register rows are not the same rows. And the legacy materiality test engaged none of the three limbs in Article 3(22), because neither MaRisk nor the Austrian provision names limbs at all. A material flag tells you which functions to assess first. It does not record the assessment.
We assessed a function as not critical before 2025. Does that still stand?
Treat it as untested. The pre-DORA exercises looked at functions that were being outsourced, and DORA looks at all functions. A negative reached under a narrower question is not a negative under a wider one. Positives are the reusable half of the legacy work.
Does a Luxembourg 22/806 classification transfer?
More cleanly than any other national scheme in this set. Circular 22/806 defines a critical or important function in terms very close to Article 3(22), including the phrase about services and activities without the narrowing qualifier other guidelines used. The work left is confirming scope and attaching assessment dates.
What does a supervisor actually want to see?
A dated assessment per function, with the reasons recorded against the limbs, and a method that someone outside the firm could apply to the same facts and reach the same answer. The second half is where legacy evidence is usually thin, because most pre-DORA schemes let each firm define its own levels.
Where do the quotations come from?
Primary texts only, read in full rather than summarised from commentary. DORA and the implementing regulation come from the EU publications office. The supervisory guidance comes from the EBA, EIOPA, ESMA, BaFin, the FMA, the CSSF and the Central Bank of Ireland, each as published by the authority itself.
